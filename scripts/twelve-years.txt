# For Twelve Years I Sent Money Home To Build My Mother A House

## a young man hugging his mother goodbye at a crowded airport departure gate, dawn light through tall windows, emotional farewell
@ a hopeful young man in his early twenties, simple shirt, carrying a worn backpack
I left home when I was twenty three. My mother cried at the gate and would not let go of my hand. I promised her I would build us a house. A real one. With a roof that did not leak.

## a young man working in a hot cramped commercial restaurant kitchen at night, steam and harsh overhead light, exhausted
@ a tired young man in a stained white kitchen uniform, exhausted expression, apron
For twelve years I worked in a kitchen that never cooled down. Fourteen hour shifts, six days a week. I shared a room with three other men. I ate what the restaurant was going to throw away.

## a man queuing at a small money transfer shop counter, fluorescent light, holding cash and a receipt
I walked to the transfer shop on the corner every month. Every month I sent home almost everything I earned. I kept enough for rent and for food. Nothing else. Not once in twelve years.

## a man looking at construction photographs on his phone screen in a dim shared bedroom at night
@ a man in his early thirties in a plain t shirt, holding a phone, quiet hopeful expression
My younger brother was handling the building. He sent me photographs every few months. The foundation. Then the walls. Then a roof. I saved every picture he ever sent me.

## a man alone crying quietly on a narrow bed in a small room, phone in hand, blue night light
I missed my father's funeral. I could not afford the flight and still send the money that month. My brother told me the house was nearly finished. He said my room faced the morning sun.

## an empty overgrown field with waist high grass and a small pile of weathered concrete blocks, grey overcast sky
@ a man in his mid thirties in a travel jacket, stunned disbelieving expression, holding a suitcase
Last March I finally went home. The taxi stopped at an empty field. Waist high grass. A pile of concrete blocks going grey. Nothing had been built there in eleven years.

## a man standing outside a nice modern house with an expensive car parked in front, tense confrontation, hard afternoon light
@ a smug well dressed man in his early thirties, expensive shirt and watch, defensive posture
My brother owned a car. A shop in the market. Two apartments he collected rent on. Every photograph he sent me for twelve years was of somebody else's house.

## an older woman sitting alone at a kitchen table looking down, unable to meet the camera, soft window light
@ an elderly woman with grey hair in a worn cardigan, ashamed sorrowful expression
My mother knew. She had known for years. She stayed quiet because she was afraid that if I found out, I would stop sending anything at all.

## a man sitting alone on a bench at dusk looking out at a wide horizon, calm and resolved, golden fading light
@ a man in his mid thirties in a plain jacket, calm resolved expression, standing tall
I got back on the plane. I have not spoken to either of them since. I am thirty five and starting again from nothing. But this time I am building it for myself.
